SwiftMiner 1.40.4

Version 1.40.4 · Build 2026090320

A campaign with more than one reward is no longer abandoned after you claim the first one. This release also stops a campaign disappearing from mining because a single Twitch response arrived incomplete, and says so in the Activity Log when it happens.

🐝 Bug fixes

Multi-reward campaigns keep being mined all the way through.

  • Claiming one reward no longer retires the whole campaign. Claiming refreshes a campaign’s details, and if that refresh came back without its reward list, SwiftMiner treated the campaign as having nothing left to earn and stopped mining it — for up to four hours, or for the rest of the campaign. A recent esports campaign lost its second, longer reward this way on three consecutive days, each time within a couple of minutes of the first reward being claimed and with about twenty-one hours of the window still to run.
  • A refresh can no longer leave a campaign knowing less than it did. Reward lists, approved-channel lists, restriction flags, and confirmed game-account links are all kept when a Twitch response omits them. Twitch omitting a field is a lost answer, not a statement that the campaign lost the thing — a finished campaign still lists its rewards, marked as claimed.
  • A campaign that returns only some of its rewards no longer loses the rest. Previously only a completely empty list was protected, so a two-tier campaign sending one tier could still erase the other.
  • A campaign that vanishes from both of Twitch’s lists while its window is still open and its rewards are unfinished is kept rather than dropped, until the window closes or your inventory confirms everything is claimed.
  • A single “not connected” answer no longer unlinks a campaign your game account is linked to. A genuine unlink is still picked up within twenty minutes.
  • A campaign listed with an unusable start or end date is now ignored rather than treated as having already expired.

🔍 Visible when it matters

The Activity Log now names the campaign instead of only lowering a count.

  • When a campaign you are mining stops being eligible while it is still active, the Activity Log names it and reports what it lost — rewards, eligibility, game-account link, status, and end time. Previously this was visible only as a number going down, which is why the problem above went unnoticed across three campaigns.
  • When a campaign is repaired from its last good state, the Activity Log says so and names the missing field. Campaigns Twitch listed with an unusable window are reported too.

⚡ Fewer repeated requests

Campaign details stay cached; only the parts that actually change are re-checked.

  • A cached campaign is served for its full lifetime, with only its game-account link re-checked on the way out. Discarding the whole entry to avoid a stale link would have sent most campaigns to a fresh network request every twenty minutes per account, on the request Twitch rate-limits hardest.
  • Remembered campaign data is compared by reward definition rather than by minute-to-minute progress, so an actively mined campaign no longer rewrites its cache file on every refresh.
  • Cache trimming runs once per refresh instead of once per campaign, and one miner’s prioritised-games choice can no longer travel through the shared cache to another.
